## 1. Descendant combinators over nesting

Documents: R rules of `g g rect` (K = 2), `g g g rect` (K = 3) or `g g g g rect` (K = 4), over a chain of D nested `<g>` with a rect in each (D = 16, 32, 64, 128), and over a flat control (one `<g>` of 10,000 rects).

Counts:

- **(a) est2**: about R x 2 x all elements x 0.0935 us (a combinator branch counts two passes); it is 0.65x to 2,285x off (gsd 8.9).

- **Exact work** (a DP that reproduces ExCssQuery's composition: the root's descendants, then per step the summed descendant counts of the current matches, duplicates included): 0.065 us, ratio 0.91 to 3.11, gsd 1.35.

The flat control is cheap (55 to 264 ms): only nesting multiplies. At 1 s of decode the depth bound is about 1.7e7; a 3.5 KB document passes 5 s.

**Can the guard count it?** Yes, cheaply. The reader pass knows each element's depth (`reader.Depth`); the stylesheet scan knows each selector branch's count of descendant steps K. The bound is R_K x sum over elements of sum over s = 1..K of C(depth, s), computed once per distinct K: O(elements x K). It needs no matching. It errs wide when ancestors do not match the left compounds (a type key per step would tighten it, at O(elements x steps) per distinct selector). Child combinators (`>`) do not multiply this way (`Child` is one level), so only descendant steps count.

| K=3 N=3,000 | 128,543 | past 60,000 (abandoned) | 3.7 | 1.35e10 |

Each step takes `Parent.Children.Skip(IndexOf + 1)` for every current match, duplicates included, so a step reads the whole sibling list per match: n^2 for one step, about n^3/2 for two. Fitted 0.0144 us per read (3 points, gsd 1.45); by it the K=3 N=3,000 document would take about 3 minutes.

**Can the guard count it?** Yes. It knows each parent's child count from the pass. With every sibling matching, the work for a rule of K - 1 sibling steps is n x sum over s = 1..K-1 of C(n, s) per parent of n children (one step: n^2; two: n x (n + n(n - 1)/2)). The bound needs only the child counts and the step count. Adjacent-sibling (`+`) takes one sibling and does not multiply.

## 4. var() fallback re-descent

Documents: `--vK: var(--v(K-1), var(--v(K-1)))` (F=1) and with two references in the fallback (F=2), for K up to D = 10, 15, 16, 20, 40, with E = 1, 10, 100 rects reading `var(--vD)`.

| document | decode ms (E = 1 / 10 / 100) | resolution calls per use (simulated) | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| F=1 D=10 | 5 / 5 / 6 | 12 | resolves |
| F=1 D=15 | 4 / 5 / 7 | 17 | resolves |
| F=1 D=16 | 6 / 15 / 88 | 6,764 | fails |
| F=1 D=20 | 5 / 8 / 36 | 6,764 | fails |
| F=1 D=40 | 6 / 8 / 37 | 6,764 | fails |
| F=2 D=10, 15 | 5 / 5 / 7 | 12, 17 | resolves |
| F=2 D=16, 20, 40 | 5 / 8 / 37 to 39 | 6,764 | fails |

(Documents 500 to 9,496 bytes.) **est2 is wrong in both directions here.** For the failing chains it gives 0.01 to 0.06 ms (it counts one copy where Svg.Custom makes 6,764 calls). For the resolving chains it gives 0.2 ms to 275 s: it counts every `var(` in a value, the fallback's included, as copies, but a lookup that succeeds never reaches its fallback. F=2 D=15 E=100 is estimated at 275 s and decodes in 7 ms; F=2 D=15 E=1, a single 800-byte element, at 2.8 s. Under a 2 s limit est2 would refuse documents like these, so the var() count must follow the resolution (the DP below), not count references.

The simulator follows the IL: a value fails past depth 32; a reference looks its name up at depth + 1 and resolves its value at depth + 2; a failed lookup (depth failures included) resolves the fallback at depth + 2; a name being resolved fails. The fallback re-descent is real, but each re-descent spends more depth, so the calls form a Fibonacci-like count bounded by the depth budget: 6,764 calls per failing use for this shape, whatever K past 15. Two references in the fallback add nothing: the first fails and the value gives up. Fitted 0.058 us per call (6 points of 20 ms or more, ratio 0.92 to 2.24, gsd 1.37): about 0.4 ms per failing use, so roughly 12,700 rects reading such a chain (about 1 MB) would take 5 s. That point was not measured.

**Can the guard count it?** Yes: a DP over (custom-property name, depth 0..32) giving the calls (and success) of resolving that name at that depth, from the definitions est2 already collects; each var() use costs the DP's value at depth 1. At most names x 33 states.
